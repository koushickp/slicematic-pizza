Part A

Product Requirements Document

Full specification — functional requirements, user stories, user flow, implementation decisions, testing strategy

Problem Statement

SliceMatic is a single-outlet pizza delivery brand in New Ashok Nagar, Delhi that currently takes all customer orders over the phone. This process is error-prone: staff mishear toppings, miscalculate prices, forget to apply discounts, and manually compute GST. There is no structured record of orders, so the owner has no data to analyse demand patterns, identify top-selling items, or calculate daily revenue accurately.

Customers experience wait times, hold music, and uncertainty about their order total before it arrives. The outlet cannot scale beyond what two people on the phone can handle.

Key Pain Points: ~15% order errors from phone miscommunication · No order history for business analysis · Manual GST calculation · Single-channel bottleneck at peak hours

Solution

A digital self-service ordering system that guides customers step-by-step through selecting a pizza base, pizza type, and topping from a menu loaded dynamically from external data files — then calculates pricing, applies quantity discounts, adds GST, collects a payment mode selection, and persists every completed order to a structured log file.

The system eliminates phone-order errors, gives customers a transparent itemised bill before they commit, and produces a machine-readable order history the owner can query for business analysis. Delivered as a Gradio MVP in Stage 2, rebuilt as a full-stack web app with Supabase and an AI feature in Stage 3.

Core Value: Validated digital intake · Auto-calculated pricing + GST + discounts · Append-only order log for BI · File-driven menu (no hardcoding) · 0 unhandled exceptions

Product Vision

SliceMatic's digital ordering system replaces phone-based order intake with a structured, self-service application for urban millennials and Gen Z customers (18–35 years) within a 5 km radius of New Ashok Nagar, Delhi. The system eliminates order errors caused by miscommunication, reduces counter staff workload, and produces a structured order history that drives operational decisions. Every completed order is persisted for business analysis, giving the outlet the data foundation it currently lacks entirely.

Functional Requirements

FR-01 Customer Onboarding
  • Collect customer full name: alphabets and spaces only, minimum 2 characters, maximum 40 characters (after stripping whitespace)
  • Collect phone number: exactly 10 digits, must begin with 6, 7, 8, or 9 (Indian mobile format)
  • On any validation failure: display a specific, human-readable error message and re-prompt. Do not crash or proceed
  • Log a session timestamp (ISO 8601) at the start of each new ordering session
FR-02 Pizza Quantity Selection
  • Accept integers from 1 to 10 inclusive. Reject floats, strings, zero, negatives, and values > 10
  • Apply 10% discount automatically when quantity >= 5. Show discount as a separate bill line item
  • Display a clear message when the discount is applied
FR-03 Menu Display & Selection — Loaded from Files
  • Load Types_of_Base.txt, Types_of_Pizza.txt, Types_of_Toppings.txt at startup
  • Format: ID;Name;Price(INR) — one item per line
  • Display each category as a numbered list with names and prices in INR
  • Accept selection by item number only. Reject out-of-range, non-integer, and empty input
  • Each order requires exactly one base, one pizza type, and one topping
  • If a file is missing or all lines malformed: display a clear error and exit gracefully
FR-04 Pricing Engine
  • Unit price = Base price + Pizza price + Topping price
  • Subtotal = Unit price × Quantity
  • If quantity >= 5: post-discount subtotal = subtotal × 0.90
  • GST = 18% applied on the post-discount subtotal (not per item)
  • Total payable = post-discount subtotal + GST
  • All prices in INR with Rs. symbol, rounded to 2 decimal places
FR-05 Order Summary & Bill Display
  • Itemised bill: Base, Pizza, Topping (name + unit price), quantity, subtotal, discount (if applicable), post-discount subtotal, GST @ 18%, Total Payable
  • Aligned columns, consistent Rs. symbols, clearly marked totals
  • Rendered in gr.Dataframe or gr.HTML — not plain text
FR-06 Payment Flow
  • Offer exactly 3 modes: 1. Cash   2. Card   3. UPI
  • Reject invalid selections with a re-prompt
  • Confirmation messages: Cash → "Pay Rs.X in cash to rider" · Card → "Your card will be charged Rs.X" · UPI → "Complete UPI payment of Rs.X to SliceMatic@upi"
FR-07 Order Persistence
  • Append every completed order to orders_log.txt immediately after payment confirmation
  • Never overwrite — append-only
  • Each record: timestamp · customer name · phone · base (ID+name+price) · pizza · topping · unit price · quantity · subtotal · discount · GST · total · payment mode
  • Format: one order per block, pipe-separated fields, blank line between orders
  • Example: TIMESTAMP|2025-06-25T14:30:00|NAME|Rajan Sharma|PHONE|9876543210|BASE|B3;Cheese Burst;229|PIZZA|P7;BBQ Chicken;379|...

Implementation Decisions

System Modules

1. Menu Loader
Reads a .txt file, parses lines in ID;Name;Price format, validates each field, returns a list of menu items. Skips malformed lines with a warning. Raises a descriptive exception if the file is missing or yields zero valid items.
2. Input Validator
Stateless functions for each input type: name, phone, quantity, menu selection, payment mode. Each returns either a validated value or a specific human-readable error string. No exceptions raised — callers receive a result/error pair.
3. Pricing Engine
Pure function: takes base price, pizza price, topping price, and quantity → returns unit price, subtotal, discount amount, post-discount subtotal, GST amount, and total payable. No I/O. No state. Fully deterministic.
4. Bill Formatter
Takes Pricing Engine output + selected item names → produces structured bill rows for gr.Dataframe or HTML table. Performs no calculation — formatting only.
5. Order Logger
Takes a completed order object → appends to orders_log.txt in pipe-separated key-value format. Catches and surfaces write errors without crashing.

Architectural Decisions

  • No hardcoded menu items. All menu data flows through the Menu Loader. Grader file swapping produces identical behaviour.
  • Business rules as named constants. DISCOUNT_THRESHOLD = 5, DISCOUNT_RATE = 0.10, GST_RATE = 0.18, PAYMENT_MODES = {1: "Cash", 2: "Card", 3: "UPI"}.
  • GST on post-discount subtotal. Order: subtotal → discount → GST → total. Never per item.
  • Gradio state machine. gr.Blocks + gr.State. Each step gated — next field only revealed after previous validates.
  • Stage 3 Supabase schema. Three tables: menu_items, orders, order_line_items. Menu loaded from DB, not files.

Sensitivity Analysis — Required Challenge Questions

Q1: Break-even if rent increases to Rs.70,000/month?
New fixed costs = Rs.2,17,910. New break-even = 338/month = 11.3 orders/day — negligible impact. Model becomes economically strained only if rent exceeds ~Rs.5.75L/month, far above any realistic New Ashok Nagar market rate.
Q2: Impact of 40% orders via Zomato/Swiggy at 25% commission?
Aggregator commission: Rs.211.75/order. Aggregator contribution margin: Rs.432.25. Blended margin (60% direct + 40% aggregator): Rs.559.3/order. New break-even: 12.1 orders/day. Recommendation: cap aggregator channel at 40% to protect margins.
Q3: At what volume does hiring a 3rd rider become justified?
Operationally required above 55 orders/day (2 riders saturate at ~60–70 trips/day). Financially: incremental revenue = Rs.1,54,560/month vs. Rs.16,000 rider cost — clearly justified at 55+ orders/day.
Q4: Is the 10% discount (qty >= 5) a value-driver or margin leak?
Contribution margin drops from 76.1% to 73.4% on a 5-pizza order (lost Rs.423.50). Verdict: Value-driver. Higher-volume orders reduce per-pizza delivery cost, improve kitchen batch efficiency, and deliver a higher absolute contribution per session (Rs.2,796.50 vs Rs.644 for a single-pizza order).
Q5: COGS +12% inflation — additional orders needed to maintain EBITDA?
Ingredient COGS rises Rs.17.76/order. New contribution margin: Rs.626.24. Monthly shortfall: Rs.25,042. Additional orders needed: ~40/month (1.3/day). The model is resilient — a 12% COGS increase requires only ~3% more volume.
Q6: Three BI metrics from order log that directly improve profitability
1. Peak-hour demand heatmap → optimize rider scheduling, reduce idle labour (save Rs.8–12K/month)
2. Top pizza × topping combos by margin → curate high-margin bundles and promotional offers (+3–5% AOV)
3. Discount order frequency as % of total → detect threshold gaming, calibrate discount policy

7. GST Treatment

Pizza home delivery attracts 18% GST in India (home delivery without composition scheme). SliceMatic is GST-registered.

  • GST is charged to the customer — collected on behalf of the government, not revenue
  • P&L computed on ex-GST amounts — GST collected is a government liability
  • Input Tax Credit (ITC) — SliceMatic claims ITC on GST paid on ingredients, packaging, equipment. Estimated benefit: Rs.18,000–22,000/month
  • Effective GST liability: Output ~Rs.1,77,564 minus ITC ~Rs.20,000 = net Rs.1,57,564/month payable
  • In ordering system: prices displayed ex-GST; GST added at bill stage on post-discount subtotal
